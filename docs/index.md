# Overview
An increasing number of modern applications of physics, chemistry, materials science, and engineering rely on materials that exhibit manifestations of quantum physics. These quantum materials hold the key not only to new advanced technologies but also to raise fundamental questions about the microscopic physical world. To understand such systems, it has become increasingly necessary to employ a combination of high-resolution spectroscopies and microscopies in parallel with theoretical modeling of the interplay of spin, charge, orbital, and lattice degrees of freedom to disentangle the rich tapestry of complex properties across spatial and temporal scales. 

The Los Alamos Computational Materials Science Summer School is an immersive ten-week program that aims to expose students to modern and emerging theoretical and experimental techniques, as well as their application to problems in the physical sciences and engineering. This summer school includes pedagogical lectures and one-on-one mentoring from Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) staff scientists who are conducting cutting-edge condensed matter physics, quantum chemistry, and materials science research.

Lectures will cover (i) the background quantum mechanical and many-body theory necessary to understand modern computational methods, (ii) introduce emerging material informatics and ML/AI tools, and (iii) in-depth tutorials on key experimental techniques, along with their theoretical underpinnings. Topics will be presented in a logical sequence to ensure all students are on the same footing. Lectures will be given by world-leading experts from academia and national laboratories in condensed matter, quantum chemistry, and material science.

Extensive hands-on training via a research project in computational material science will be an integral part of the school. Each student will be paired with a LANL mentor who will propose project topics and provide guidance. Each project will involve hands-on theoretical and/or computational work and use of the LANL High Performance Computing facility. *If time permits*, the students will begin preparing their results for publication.

Additionally, the students will visit three user facilities to show how the techniques learned in the classroom translate to real scientific problems. 

This school is open to theoretical, computational, and experimental science students who have completed at least one year of graduate work and are familiar with advanced quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics. Each student will have the opportunity to present their work from their home institution to kick-off the school and present a talk at the close of the school on their summer research. Both events, along with various social activities, e.g., BBQs, Hikes, picnics, are open to all students, visiting faculty, and the whole LANL community to encourage in-depth discussion and networking. This school will help train the next generation of researchers to use and develop these tools and methods in their own research programs. 

We note that both US citizens and non-US citizens are eligible to apply (citizenship is not a requirement).

Registration is required for non-LANL participants who will receive a fellowship stipend to cover living expenses and travel.
